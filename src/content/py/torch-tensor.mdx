Two differences from NumPy that will bite you early.

The default float type is `float32`, not `float64`. This is deliberate —
half the memory, and roughly twice the throughput on hardware that cares —
but it means `torch.tensor([1, 2, 3])` gives you an *integer* tensor, and
passing that where a float is expected fails in a confusing way. Write
`torch.tensor([1., 2., 3.])` or pass `dtype=torch.float32`.

Tensors carry a device. Two tensors on different devices cannot be combined,
and the error says so plainly. `t.to(device)` moves one; the idiom is to
choose the device once at the top of a script and route everything through it.
