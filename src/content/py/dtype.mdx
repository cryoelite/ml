Write `np.array([1, 2, 3])` and you get integers. Write `np.array([1., 2., 3.])`
— three extra characters — and you get floats. Everything downstream depends on
which one you did.

The failure mode is not an error. Assigning `2.7` into an integer array truncates
to `2` without a word, and a gradient step that should have moved a weight by
0.003 moves it by zero, forever.

Three habits that remove the whole category:

- Write `1.` rather than `1` when you mean a float. Always.
- Pass `dtype=` explicitly when you build an array that matters.
- Print `.dtype` alongside `.shape` when something is behaving strangely.

PyTorch inherits the same trap and adds one of its own: its default float is
`float32`, not `float64`, so a NumPy array converted with `torch.from_numpy` may
arrive as `float64` and then refuse to multiply with a model's `float32` weights.
The fix is `.float()`, and you will type it many times.
