title: 'the axis argument'
lib: 'numpy'
summary: '`axis=k` means ''collapse dimension k''. The result has that dimension removed.'
signature: 'a.sum(axis=0)   |   a.mean(axis=1)   |   a.argmax(axis=-1)'
example: |-
  a = np.arange(6).reshape(2, 3)
  a.sum(axis=0), a.sum(axis=1)
output: '(array([3, 5, 7]), array([ 3, 12]))'
rust: '`iter().map(...).sum()` over one dimension of a nested structure — but which one is chosen by an integer rather than by which loop you wrote.'
docs: 'https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.sum.html'
docsLabel: 'numpy.sum ↗'
---

The reliable way to read it: **`axis=k` is the dimension that disappears.**
For an array of shape `(2, 3)`, `sum(axis=0)` removes the 2 and leaves shape
`(3,)`; `sum(axis=1)` removes the 3 and leaves `(2,)`.

In practice `axis=0` almost always means *down the rows, across the batch* —
"the mean of this feature over all examples" — and `axis=-1` means *along the
last dimension*, which is where the classes live in a batch of predictions.
`preds.argmax(axis=-1)` is how a matrix of class scores becomes a vector of
predicted labels.

Add `keepdims=True` when you want the result to keep its rank so it can
broadcast back against the original. That is the standard trick inside
softmax.
