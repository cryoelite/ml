---
title: 'requires_grad / backward'
lib: 'torch'
summary: 'Tensors record the operations applied to them; `.backward()` replays that tape to fill in `.grad`.'
signature: |-
  x = torch.tensor(2.0, requires_grad=True)
  loss.backward()   ->   x.grad
example: |-
  x = torch.tensor(3.0, requires_grad=True)
  y = x ** 2
  y.backward()
  x.grad
output: 'tensor(6.)'
rust: 'No analogue in std. It is operator overloading that, as a side effect, builds a DAG of the computation, then walks it backwards applying the chain rule. `dfdx` and `burn` do the same thing with types instead of a runtime tape.'
docs: 'https://pytorch.org/docs/stable/notes/autograd.html'
docsLabel: 'Autograd mechanics ↗'
---

$y = x^2$, so $dy/dx = 2x$, and at $x = 3$ that is 6. PyTorch did not do
algebra to get this — it recorded that a squaring happened and applied the
known derivative of squaring in reverse.

Gradients **accumulate** rather than overwrite. That is a feature (it lets you
split a large batch across several backward passes) and a trap: forgetting
`optimiser.zero_grad()` means every step uses the sum of all gradients so far,
and training diverges for no visible reason. See
[Backpropagation](/learn/09-backpropagation/).
