Two facts do most of the work.

**It is one flat buffer.** A `(2, 3)` array is six contiguous doubles plus the
metadata `shape=(2,3)`. `reshape` and most slicing therefore cost nothing —
they hand back a new view over the same memory. This is why you can afford to
reshape constantly.

**Every element has the same type.** That is what lets the loop live in C
instead of in the interpreter. A Python `list` of a million floats is a
million pointers to a million boxed objects; a `float64` array of a million
elements is eight megabytes and one pointer. The performance gap is roughly
fifty-fold, and it is the entire reason NumPy exists.
