summary: 'Squash every value into a range. Mostly used to stop a logarithm meeting a zero.'
signature: 'np.clip(a, low, high)'
example: |-
  p = np.array([0.0, 0.5, 1.0])
  -np.log(np.clip(p, 1e-12, 1 - 1e-12))   # safe cross-entropy
output: 'array([2.76310211e+01, 6.93147181e-01, 1.00000008e-12])'

The one use you will reach for constantly: `log(0)` is `-inf`, and one `inf` in a
loss becomes `nan` on the next update and never leaves. Clipping probabilities
away from exactly 0 and exactly 1 makes cross-entropy safe for the price of an
error around $10^{-12}$, which is nothing.

It also appears as **gradient clipping** — bound the size of an update so one bad
batch cannot blow up the weights — and as a way to bound an activation.

If you find yourself clipping the *output* of a model to keep it sensible, that
is worth a second thought. It is often a sign the model should have been
predicting something else, like a log, that cannot go out of range in the first
place.
