---
title: 'df.loc / df.iloc'
lib: 'pandas'
summary: '`loc` selects by label, `iloc` by integer position. Mixing them up is the classic pandas bug.'
signature: 'df.loc[row_label, col_label]   |   df.iloc[row_pos, col_pos]'
example: |-
  df.loc[df['age'] > 30, ['city']]
  df.iloc[0:2, 0]
rust: '`iloc` is ordinary slice indexing. `loc` is closer to a `HashMap` lookup that happens to accept a boolean mask, a list of keys, or a range — which is why it is powerful and why it surprises people.'
docs: 'https://pandas.pydata.org/docs/user_guide/indexing.html'
docsLabel: 'Indexing guide ↗'
---

The trap: `loc` slices are **inclusive** of the end label, because labels are
not necessarily numbers and "up to but not including" is meaningless for
strings. `iloc` slices are exclusive, like every other Python slice. So
`df.loc[0:2]` gives three rows and `df.iloc[0:2]` gives two.

Use `loc` with a boolean mask for filtering — `df.loc[df.age > 30]` — and
reach for `iloc` mainly when you really mean "the first n rows".
