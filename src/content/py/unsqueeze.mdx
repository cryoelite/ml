Look hard at that example, because it is the bug.

`nn.Linear(h, 1)` outputs `(n, 1)`. Your labels are `(n,)`. Subtracting them
does not fail — it [broadcasts](/appendix/python/#broadcasting) into `(n, n)`,
and your loss becomes the mean of $n^2$ meaningless numbers. The model trains.
The loss goes down. Nothing is right.

`squeeze()` removes every dimension of size 1; `squeeze(dim)` removes only the
one you name, which is safer — a batch of size 1 would otherwise vanish along
with the dimension you meant.

`unsqueeze(dim)` inserts one. NumPy spells the same operation `arr[:, None]`, and
you will see both.

The habit that prevents the bug entirely: print the shape of your predictions and
your labels, side by side, before your first training run.
