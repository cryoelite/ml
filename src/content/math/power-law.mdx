A power law has the form $y = a x^{-\alpha}$. Plotting both axes on a logarithmic scale turns this relationship into a straight line.

Some language-model experiments have found power-law relationships between loss and model size, data, or compute over tested ranges. This helped researchers estimate how training loss might change as resources increased. The relationship depends on the training setup and should not be treated as a universal law.

Later work showed that compute-efficient training depends on balancing model size and the number of training tokens, not only increasing parameters. See [Kaplan et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) and [Hoffmann et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556).

A lower language-model loss also does not guarantee a predictable change in every downstream capability.
