Squaring does two jobs. It removes the sign, so errors cannot cancel out. And
it punishes large errors disproportionately — being wrong by 10 is a hundred
times worse than being wrong by 1, not ten times.

That second property is a modelling decision, not a mathematical necessity. If
your data has outliers you may not want the model to contort itself to
accommodate them, in which case mean absolute error or Huber loss is the
better choice. Choosing the loss *is* choosing what "wrong" means.

Squaring also happens to make the derivative pleasant, which mattered a great
deal before automatic differentiation and matters much less now.
