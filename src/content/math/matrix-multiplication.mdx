title: 'Matrix multiplication'
summary: 'Every entry of the result is the dot product of a row of the left matrix with a column of the right.'
formula: '(AB)_{ij} = \sum_{k} A_{ik} B_{kj} \qquad (n{\times}k)(k{\times}m) \to (n{\times}m)'
docs: 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrix_multiplication'
docsLabel: 'Wikipedia ↗'
symbols:
  - sym: 'A_{ik}'
    means: 'row i, column k of A'
  - sym: 'k'
    means: 'the shared inner dimension — it is summed away'
  - sym: 'n \times m'
    means: 'the shape of the result'
---

The shape rule is worth more than the formula: **the inner dimensions must
match, and they vanish.** $(32, 784) \times (784, 128) \to (32, 128)$ reads as
"32 examples of 784 features each, through a layer with 128 outputs".

A neural network layer *is* a matrix multiply. The reason deep learning
arrived when it did is that GPUs happen to be machines built to do this one
operation extremely fast, for reasons that had nothing to do with machine
learning.
