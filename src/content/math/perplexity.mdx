Language model papers report perplexity rather than loss, because exponentiating
turns an abstract number into an intuitive one.

**Perplexity is the effective number of equally-likely options the model is
choosing between at each step.** A model with a 50,000-token vocabulary and a
perplexity of 50,000 has learned nothing at all. A perplexity of 20 means it is
about as uncertain as picking among twenty words — which is a good model.

The practical value of the exponential is that it makes improvements legible. A
loss going from 3.00 to 2.89 sounds negligible; the same change in perplexity is
20.1 to 18.0, which is visibly a tenth of the remaining uncertainty removed.

Two cautions when comparing numbers across papers. Perplexity depends on the
**tokeniser** — a model with a larger vocabulary sees fewer, harder tokens and
its perplexity is not comparable. And it depends on the evaluation corpus, so a
number quoted without one means nothing.
