$\sigma(0) = 0.5$, $\sigma(2) \approx 0.88$, $\sigma(-2) \approx 0.12$, and it
never quite reaches 0 or 1. Large positive scores saturate near 1, large
negative near 0.

A linear model plus a sigmoid is logistic regression, which despite the name
is a classifier and remains the correct first thing to try on a binary
problem.

Its derivative is the delightfully compact $\sigma(z)(1 - \sigma(z))$ — but
notice that this is at most $0.25$, and near-zero once saturated. Multiply a
few of those through the chain rule and the gradient dies. That is the
vanishing-gradient problem, and it is why hidden layers now use
[ReLU](/appendix/math/#relu) instead.
