The formula is less useful than the picture: a 3x3 grid of weights slides over
an image, and at each position you multiply the nine weights by the nine
pixels underneath and sum. The same nine weights are used at every position.

That reuse is the entire idea. A vertical-edge detector is a vertical-edge
detector wherever it is applied, so the network learns nine numbers instead of
nine per pixel. This is *weight sharing*, and it encodes a genuine assumption
about images — that what a thing looks like does not depend on where it is.

When that assumption is false, convolutions are the wrong tool, which is
precisely why they never worked well on tabular data.
