Not required for anything in this tutorial, and included because the vertical
bar is everywhere in the literature and is worth being able to read.

It underpins an entire parallel tradition — naive Bayes, Gaussian processes,
variational inference, Bayesian neural networks — whose selling point is
calibrated uncertainty: not just a prediction but an honest statement of how
sure the model is. That tradition lost the scaling race and is very much alive
wherever data is scarce or being wrong is expensive.

It is also the reason a language model's objective is written
$P(\text{next token} \mid \text{everything so far})$.
