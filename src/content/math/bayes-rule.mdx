Bayes’ rule updates a probability after you observe evidence. Read $P(H \mid E)$ as “the probability of hypothesis H given evidence E.”

The prior $P(H)$ describes what was plausible before the evidence. The likelihood $P(E \mid H)$ describes how likely the evidence would be under that hypothesis. The posterior is the updated probability.

Bayesian methods use this update in models such as naive Bayes and Gaussian processes. The same notation appears in language models: the probability of the next token given the tokens so far.
