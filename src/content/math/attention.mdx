docs: 'https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762'

symbols:
  - sym: 'Q'
    means: 'queries: what each position is looking for'
  - sym: 'K'
    means: 'keys: what each position offers'
  - sym: 'V'
    means: 'values: what each position actually contributes'
  - sym: 'K^{\top}'
    means: 'K transposed, so QK-transpose is every query against every key'
  - sym: '\sqrt{d_k}'

---

Read it right to left, and it stops being intimidating.

$QK^{\top}$ is every query dotted with every key — a square grid of "how
relevant is position j to position i". Dividing by $\sqrt{d_k}$ keeps those
numbers from growing with the embedding size, which would push softmax into
its saturated region where gradients vanish. Softmax turns each row into
weights that sum to one. Multiplying by $V$ takes the corresponding weighted
average.

So: *each position produces a query, compares it against every key, and mixes
the values in proportion to how well they matched.* That is the whole
mechanism, and it is the load-bearing idea of every model you currently think
of as AI.

The cost is that the grid is $n \times n$: doubling the context length
quadruples the work. Nearly all research on long context is an attack on that
one quadratic.
