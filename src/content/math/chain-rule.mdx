This single rule is why deep learning works. A neural network *is* a deep
nesting of functions: $L(f_n(\dots f_2(f_1(x))))$. The chain rule says the
derivative of the whole stack is the product of the derivatives of the parts,
and that each part only needs to know its own local derivative.

Read it as a conversion of units. If the loss changes 3 units per unit of $g$,
and $g$ changes 2 units per unit of $x$, then the loss changes $3 \times 2 = 6$
units per unit of $x$. Backpropagation is this multiplication carried out
layer by layer, right to left.

It also explains the classic failure mode. Multiply thirty numbers that are
each around 0.5 and you get $10^{-9}$: the gradient vanishes, and early layers
stop learning. ReLU, residual connections and normalisation layers are all, in
part, answers to that arithmetic.
