formula: '\theta_{t+1} = \theta_t - \eta \, \nabla_\theta L(\theta_t)'

symbols:

    means: 'the parameters at step t'
  - sym: '\eta'
    means: '''eta'' — the learning rate, the size of the step'
  - sym: '-'

  - sym: '\nabla_\theta L'

Every optimiser you will meet is a variation on this line. Momentum adds a
running average of past gradients so the step keeps its direction through
noise. Adam additionally divides by a running estimate of each gradient's
magnitude, giving every parameter its own effective step size. RMSProp is Adam
without the momentum term.

$\eta$ is the hyperparameter that matters most. Too large and the loss
diverges to NaN; too small and training takes a week to get nowhere. The
practical method is to try a few on a log scale — $10^{-1}, 10^{-2}, 10^{-3}$ —
and watch which loss curve falls fastest without exploding.
