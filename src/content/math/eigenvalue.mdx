An eigenvector of a square matrix is a direction the matrix preserves when it transforms a vector. The corresponding eigenvalue tells you the scale and sign of that change:

$$A v = \lambda v$$

If the same matrix is applied repeatedly, a component along an eigenvector is multiplied by $\lambda$ each time. Its magnitude tends to shrink when $|\lambda| < 1$ and grow when $|\lambda| > 1$.

The largest eigenvalue magnitude is called the spectral radius. Eigenvalues also appear in PCA and in analyses of how gradients change through recurrent networks.
