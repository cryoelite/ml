The singular value decomposition writes a matrix as $A=U\Sigma V^\top$. The matrices $U$ and $V$ describe orthogonal directions; the diagonal entries of $\Sigma$ show how much each direction is scaled.

Keeping only the largest singular values gives a best low-rank approximation under common least-squares criteria. SVD is used in PCA, compression, and low-rank methods such as LoRA.

For numerical stability, use a standard SVD solver instead of forming and inverting $X^\top X$ when solving least-squares problems.
