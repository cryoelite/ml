High bias is a straight line through a curve: it is wrong the same way every
time, and more data will not save it. High variance is a degree-20 polynomial
through nine points: it nails the training set and flails between the points,
and would look completely different given nine other points.

The third term is the honest one. If two identical inputs have different
labels — and in the real world they routinely do — no model can be right about
both. Knowing roughly how large $\sigma^2$ is for your problem is what stops
you burning a fortnight chasing an accuracy that was never available.

Note that very large neural networks visibly break the classical picture of
this tradeoff, which is an active and genuinely unsettled area. See
[Generalisation](/learn/06-generalisation/).
