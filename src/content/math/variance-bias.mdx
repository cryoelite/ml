The bias–variance decomposition separates expected squared error into bias squared, variance across training samples, and noise:

$$\mathbb{E}[(\hat{f}-y)^2] = \text{Bias}^2 + \text{Variance} + \sigma^2$$

High bias means the model’s average predictions miss the pattern. High variance means predictions change substantially when the training sample changes. Noise is variation the available inputs cannot explain under the model assumptions.

The balance between bias and variance can help explain overfitting. The classic picture is not the whole story for modern overparameterised models. See [Generalisation](/learn/06-generalisation/) and the [bias–variance extra](/extras/bias-variance/).
