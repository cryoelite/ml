Maximum likelihood chooses parameters that assign high probability to the observed data:

$$\hat{\theta} = \arg\max_\theta \sum_{i=1}^n \log p_\theta(y_i \mid x_i)$$

Taking the logarithm turns a product of probabilities into a sum and avoids numerical underflow. Maximising log-likelihood is equivalent to minimising its negative, called the negative log-likelihood.

Different assumptions about the target distribution produce different losses. A Gaussian model gives squared error; a categorical model gives cross-entropy. See [how the losses are derived](/extras/maximum-likelihood/).
