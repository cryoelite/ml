This is where the loss functions come from, rather than being invented. Assume
your targets are normally distributed around the model's prediction, write
down the likelihood, take the log — and
[mean squared error](/appendix/math/#mse) falls out. Assume they are
categorical and you get
[cross-entropy](/appendix/math/#cross-entropy) instead.

Maximising a log-likelihood and minimising a loss are the same activity with
opposite signs. Papers switch between the two framings without comment, which
is worth knowing before it confuses you.

The log is there for two practical reasons: multiplying ten thousand
probabilities underflows to zero in floating point, and sums differentiate more
pleasantly than products.
