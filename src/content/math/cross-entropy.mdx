docs: 'https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-entropy'
docsLabel: 'Wikipedia ↗'
symbols:
  - sym: 'y_c'
    means: '1 for the true class, 0 for every other — so the sum has one surviving term'
  - sym: '\hat{y}_c'
    means: 'the probability the model assigned to class c'
  - sym: '-\log'
    means: 'small when the probability is near 1, exploding as it approaches 0'

The right-hand form is the one to hold in your head: **cross-entropy is minus
the log of the probability you gave to the right answer.** Everything else is
bookkeeping.

Assign 0.9 to the correct class and you pay $-\log 0.9 = 0.105$. Assign 0.1 and
you pay $2.30$ — twenty times more. Assign 0.001 and you pay $6.9$. The curve
goes to infinity as your probability goes to zero, which is precisely the
desired incentive: a model that is confidently wrong should be punished far
more than one that was merely unsure.

This is why classifiers output probabilities via
[softmax](/appendix/math/#softmax) rather than a bare class label. A label
cannot be graded on how nearly right it was.
