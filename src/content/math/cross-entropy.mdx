For a classification example, cross-entropy is the negative log of the probability assigned to the correct class:

$$L = -\log \hat{y}_{\text{correct}}$$

A higher probability for the correct answer gives a lower loss. Assigning a very small probability gives a large penalty.

Cross-entropy can use the full target distribution when labels are not one-hot. Classifiers often produce probabilities with [softmax](/appendix/math/#softmax), but training libraries usually take logits directly for numerical stability.
