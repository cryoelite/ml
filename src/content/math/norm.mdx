Norms turn a whole vector into one number, which is what you need whenever
something must be compared or penalised.

Added to a loss as a penalty on the weights, they become regularisation. L2
(weight decay, ridge) shrinks all weights smoothly toward zero. L1 (lasso)
drives many of them to exactly zero, which makes it a feature selector as well
as a regulariser — a difference that comes from the shape of the constraint
region, and one worth seeing drawn once.

Gradient clipping is the same tool used defensively: if $\|\nabla\|_2$ exceeds
a threshold, scale the whole gradient down. It is what stops one pathological
batch from destroying an hour of training.
