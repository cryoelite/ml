The Kullback–Leibler divergence measures how predictions from one distribution differ from a reference distribution:

$$D_{KL}(p \parallel q) = \sum_i p_i \log \frac{p_i}{q_i}$$

It is zero when the distributions match and non-negative otherwise. It is not symmetric: switching p and q can change the result.

Cross-entropy decomposes as $H(p,q)=H(p)+D_{KL}(p\parallel q)$. Since $H(p)$ does not depend on the model, minimising cross-entropy also minimises the KL divergence in that direction.

KL divergence is used in variational methods and model distillation. Its direction matters, and its effects depend on the distributions and objective.
