This notation is why papers look harder than they are. When a paper writes the
training objective as
$\min_\theta \mathbb{E}_{(x,y) \sim D}[L(f_\theta(x), y)]$, it means: *make the
average loss over your data small*. That is all. The expectation is over the
data distribution, and the average over your actual dataset is the estimate of
it you can compute.

Learning to read $\mathbb{E}$ as "average over" removes most of the intimidation
from a methods section.
