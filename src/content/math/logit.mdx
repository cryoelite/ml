The logit is the **log-odds**: the log of the ratio of "happens" to "doesn't". A
logit of 0 means even odds; positive means more likely than not; large negative
means very unlikely.

It is the natural output space for a linear model, because it is unbounded — a
weighted sum can produce any real number, and the logit scale accepts all of
them. The [sigmoid](/appendix/math/#sigmoid) then maps it back to a probability.

The practical rule that follows, and it is one you will use constantly:

**Your model outputs logits. The loss function owns the squashing.**

`nn.BCEWithLogitsLoss` and `nn.CrossEntropyLoss` apply the sigmoid or softmax
internally, using a numerically stable formulation that never evaluates
`exp(large)`. Apply the squashing yourself and then use `BCELoss` and you get the
same answer on easy data and `nan` on hard data — the worst kind of bug, since it
passes testing and fails in production.

Convert to probabilities only at inference, when a human or a threshold actually
needs one.
