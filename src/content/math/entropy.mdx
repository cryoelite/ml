Entropy is the average surprise of a probability distribution. For an event with probability $p$, its information is $-\log_2 p$ bits. A certain event gives no new information; a rare event gives more.

A fair coin has one bit of entropy. A coin that lands heads 90% of the time has lower entropy because its outcome is easier to predict.

In information theory, entropy sets a lower bound on the average number of bits needed to encode outcomes from a known distribution. Decision-tree algorithms can use entropy to measure how mixed the labels are in a group.
