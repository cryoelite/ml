The information in a single event of probability $p$ is $-\log_2 p$ bits: a
certain event tells you nothing, a one-in-a-thousand event tells you about ten
bits' worth. Entropy is the average of that over a whole distribution.

A fair coin has entropy exactly 1 bit — that is the definition of a bit. A coin
that lands heads 90% of the time has 0.47 bits, because you can usually guess.
A certain outcome has zero.

The reason it matters beyond aesthetics is Shannon's source coding theorem:
**entropy is the floor on average compressed size.** No encoding can do better,
and good ones approach it.

You have already used this without the name. A decision tree's "impurity" is
entropy, and information gain is how much a split reduces it. And the
[irreducible error](/extras/bias-variance/) that no model can remove is the same
idea for a continuous target.
